> Method note: who.int, paho.org and weekly.chinacdc.cn could not be opened from this session (DNS blocked), so the WHO and China CDC figures below come from search-engine extracts of those pages and from reputable secondary reports, not from the full primary texts. ## Q1. How much does the world drink, and what is the burden? (WHO 2024 GSR, GBD, youth, market trends)

### Takeaway
WHO's 2024 global status report (2019 data) attributes **2.6 million deaths a year (4.7% of all deaths)** to alcohol. About 2.0 million of those deaths are men. An estimated **209 million people** live with alcohol dependence. Global per-capita consumption has fallen only slightly, from 5.7 L in 2010 to 5.5 L in 2019. In high-income markets, demand is now visibly falling: Gallup measured 54% of US adults drinking in both 2025 and 2026, a record low, and alcohol volumes in the top 15 markets fell 3% in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**WHO Global status report on alcohol and health and treatment of substance use disorders (published 25 June 2024, 2019 data)**
- Alcohol caused 2.6 million deaths, 4.7% of all global deaths. Of these, about 2.0 million were men. Together with drugs (0.6 million), the total exceeds 3 million deaths — [WHO news release, 2024-06-25](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-06-2024-over-3-million-annual-deaths-due-to-alcohol-and-drug-use-majority-among-men); [News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240625/WHO-report-highlights-global-burden-of-alcohol-and-drug-related-deaths.aspx)
- Cause of death: about 1.6 million deaths were from noncommunicable diseases, including 474,000 from cardiovascular disease and 401,000 from cancer. About 724,000 were injury deaths (traffic, self-harm, interpersonal violence) — [PAHO/WHO release](https://www.paho.org/en/news/25-6-2024-over-3-million-annual-deaths-due-alcohol-and-drug-use-majority-among-men); [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/urgent-global-action-is-needed-to-address-alcohol-and-drug-consumption/)
- People aged 20–39 have the highest share of their deaths caused by alcohol, at 13% — [PAHO/WHO](https://www.paho.org/en/news/25-6-2024-over-3-million-annual-deaths-due-alcohol-and-drug-use-majority-among-men)
- An estimated 400 million people have alcohol or drug use disorders, of whom 209 million have alcohol dependence — [WHO release](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-06-2024-over-3-million-annual-deaths-due-to-alcohol-and-drug-use-majority-among-men)
- Total per-capita consumption (age 15+) fell from 5.7 L in 2010 to 5.5 L in 2019. Drinkers average 27 g of pure alcohol a day, about two standard drinks. Regional per-capita consumption was highest in the WHO European Region (9.2 L) and the Americas (7.5 L) — [WHO release](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-06-2024-over-3-million-annual-deaths-due-to-alcohol-and-drug-use-majority-among-men)
- Treatment gap: in countries that report data, the share of people with a substance use disorder in contact with treatment ranged from below 1% to at most 35% — [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/urgent-global-action-is-needed-to-address-alcohol-and-drug-consumption/)

**GBD (IHME) alcohol research**
- GBD 2020 alcohol paper (Lancet, July 2022, 204 countries): **1.34 billion people** drank harmful amounts in 2020. About 60% of them were aged 15–39 and more than 76% were men. In every region, the largest group of hazardous drinkers was men aged 15–39 — [UW Newsroom](https://newsroom.uw.edu/news/no-safe-level-alcohol-scientific-study-concludes); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/07/15/alcohol-study-lancet-young-adults-should-not-drink-bill-melinda-gates-foundation/amp)
- The same study found no health benefit from drinking in people aged 15–39, only risk. Their theoretical minimum risk level was about 0.1 standard drink a day. For ages 40–64, the minimum risk level was about 0.5 to nearly 2 standard drinks a day, depending on region and sex — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/07/15/alcohol-study-lancet-young-adults-should-not-drink-bill-melinda-gates-foundation/amp); [WION](https://www.wionews.com/world/how-much-alcohol-to-drink-at-what-age-the-lancet-study-reveals-497554). The "no safe level" framing has been methodologically contested — [Snowdon commentary](https://snowdon.substack.com/p/is-drinking-never-good-for-people)
- In GBD 2019, alcohol ranked 9th of 87 risk factors globally and 6th among men. In China it ranked 8th overall and 5th among men — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9247746)

**Youth drinking**
- ESPAD: past-30-day drinking among European 15–16-year-olds fell from 69% in 1995 to 36% in 2015 — [Drugs and Alcohol Ireland / Doyle](https://www.drugsandalcohol.ie/37095)
- HBSC/ESPAD data from 2018–2019 suggest the decline is slowing in some countries. A Lisbon Addictions 2022 analysis of six ESPAD waves (1999–2019) found the decline uneven, with some subgroups improving less — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612362); [Lisbon Addictions 2022](https://2022.lisbonaddictions.eu/node/4690.html); [Nordic POPNAD](https://nordicwelfare.org/popnad/en/artiklar/explanations-and-implications-of-the-decline-in-adolescents-drinking-in-the-nordic-countries-findings-from-a-comparative-project/)

**United States: Gallup**
- July 2025: 54% of US adults said they drink, the lowest since the question began in 1939 (prior low 55% in 1958). The series was 62% in 2023, 58% in 2024 and 54% in 2025. 53% now consider moderate drinking (1–2 drinks a day) harmful to health. Among ages 18–34, 50% drink, down from 59% in 2023. Drinkers average 2.8 drinks a week — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/467507/record-low-say-drink-alcohol.aspx); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/13/alcohol-drinking-rate-low-health); [Michigan Public/NPR](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2025-08-13/percentage-of-americans-who-say-they-drink-alcohol-hits-record-low-gallup-says)
- July 2026 (n≈1,200, July 1–19): still 54%, tying the record low. By income, 43% drink in households under $50k and 66% at $100k or more. 13% say they sometimes drink too much, also a record low. 17% chose a non-alcoholic drink instead of alcohol in the past year. The partisan split is reported inconsistently between outlets — [NACS/Convenience.org](https://convenience.org/stay-current/news/2026/august/31/gallup-drinking-remains-at-record-low); [Gallup topline PDF](https://news.gallup.com/file/poll/713558/20260820_Consumption%20Habits%20Topline_Alcohol.pdf); [Vinetur](https://www.vinetur.com/en/20260909106833/gallup-finds-54-of-americans-now-drink-alcohol-matching-a-record-low.html)

**Industry and market trends**
- IWSR: total alcohol volume in the 15 largest markets fell 3% in 2025, after −2% in 2024. Much of the 2025 decline came from Baby Boomers cutting back. Gen Z drinking participation (past 6 months) rose from 66% in March 2023 to 73% in March 2025, and was 74% in March 2026 against 76% for all adults. This contradicts the simple story that Gen Z does not drink — [Vinetur citing IWSR](https://www.vinetur.com/en/20260723104731/baby-boomers-drove-a-3-drop-in-global-alcohol-volumes-in-2025.html); [The Spirit Journal](https://thespiritjournal.substack.com/p/is-gen-z-really-drinking-less-or)
- IWSR forecast: no/low-alcohol volumes grow at about 4% CAGR to 2028, versus about 1% for total alcohol. No-alcohol alone grows at about 7% CAGR (2024–2028), while low-alcohol is flat — [BeverageDaily](https://beveragedaily.com/Article/2025/01/29/global-low-and-no-alcohol-market-data-for-2025)

### Inferences
- Global per-capita consumption is close to flat, falling only 3.5% between 2010 and 2019, which is far from the "20% relative reduction by 2030" target. The demand decline in high-income countries is offset by consumption in middle-income countries.
- About one in eight deaths at ages 20–39 is caused by alcohol. Because alcohol kills young people, its share of DALYs and YLLs is larger than its 4.7% share of deaths.
- The US and IWSR data suggest the recent fall in consumption is partly cyclical (inflation, disposable income) and partly structural (health perceptions, substitution by non-alcoholic drinks and cannabis). Attribution is still contested.

### Gaps
- I could not read WHO's full report table for regional deaths per litre (the African and European regions are reported to have the highest alcohol death rates) or the per-capita consumption of drinkers by region. Search extracts did not give exact numbers.
- I found no GBD 2023 (published 2025) update of the global alcohol-attributable death count. GBD and WHO totals differ by method.
- The HBSC 2021/22 wave results were not retrieved.

## Q2. China: consumption, baijiu, AUD, ALDH2, dietary guidelines and drink-driving law

### Takeaway
According to China CDC's 2024 national survey, **27.6% of people aged 15+ drank in the past year and 20.3% in the past month**. Over 40% of current drinkers engage in heavy episodic drinking. These rates are clearly below 2018 levels, although the 2018 sample covered ages 18+, so the comparison is not exact. Industrial baijiu output in 2025 was about 3.549 million kL, down 12.1% year on year and about 74% below the 2016 peak. The 2022 dietary guidelines cap adult intake at 15 g of alcohol a day for men and women alike. Drink-driving is defined at a blood alcohol concentration of 20 mg/100 mL and drunk driving at 80 mg/100 mL, which is a criminal offence (dangerous driving). Genetic (ALDH2/ADH1B) Mendelian randomisation studies show that alcohol raises stroke risk in a dose-dependent way and that the "moderate drinking protects" association is spurious.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence and consumption**
- China CDC Weekly 2026 (national multistage stratified cluster sample, age 15+): in 2024, past-month drinking was 20.3% and past-year drinking 27.6%, both higher among men and young and middle-aged adults. Over 40% of current drinkers engage in heavy episodic drinking (HED). In 2018, among adults aged 18+, past-month drinking was 28.3% and past-year drinking 39.8% — [China CDC Weekly 2026, doi:10.46234/ccdcw2026.006](https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/en/article/doi/10.46234/ccdcw2026.006); [PDF](https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/fileCCDCW/journal/article/ccdcw/2026/2/PDF/CCDCW250442.pdf)
- WHO data: China's per-capita consumption rose from 4.1 L in 2005 to 7.2 L in 2016, according to the GSR 2018 — [MDPI IJERPH review](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/19/10/5866). Another study (Manthey et al., Lancet 2019) reported a rise from an average of 4.9 L in 2003–2005 to 6.7 L in the following five years — [Jakarta Post/AFP](https://www.thejakartapost.com/life/2019/05/08/china-india-global-booze-alcohol-binge-study)
- Some analyses report that adult per-capita consumption in China fell by about 2.4 L (−33%) between 2016 and 2019. That figure came through a secondary wiki-type source, so treat it with caution — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_alcohol_consumption_per_capita). An older review characterised China's alcohol consumption growth as among the fastest in the world and its policy (taxes, drink-driving, sales to minors, marketing) as relatively weak — [Bulletin WHO 2013/PMC3629448](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3629448); [PMC9141045 narrative review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9141045/)

**Baijiu industry**
- National Bureau of Statistics data: 2025 industrial baijiu output (converted to 65% ABV, commodity volume) was about 3.549 million kL, down 12.1% year on year. December alone was 376,000 kL, down 19.0%. The 2016 peak was 13.584 million kL, so cumulative output has fallen by nearly 74%. Output has declined for six consecutive years — [观研天下/中国报告网](https://www.chinabaogao.com/data/202512/774571.html); [证券之星](https://wap.stockstar.com/detail/IG2026012800020041). Note that 证券之星 states the peak figure inconsistently; the NBS original text was not checked.

**Genetics (ALDH2/ADH1B)**
- China Kadoorie Biobank (Millwood et al., Lancet 2019): about 512,000 adults followed for about 10 years, 161,498 of them genotyped for ALDH2-rs671 and ADH1B-rs1229984. 33% of men but only 2% of women drank in most weeks. Conventional analysis shows a U-shaped curve. Genotype-predicted alcohol intake, however, is positively associated with stroke: intracerebral haemorrhage RR 1.58 and ischaemic stroke RR 1.27 per 280 g/week, with no significant association with myocardial infarction. The authors conclude that the apparent protective effect of moderate drinking comes mainly from reverse causation and confounding — [PMC6497989](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6497989); [TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/new-genetic-evidence-shows-alcoholic-drinks-dont-cut-stroke-risk)

**Dietary guidelines**
- 《中国居民膳食指南（2022）》 (Chinese Dietary Guidelines 2022): adults should consume no more than 15 g of alcohol a day, with no sex difference. Children, adolescents, pregnant women, breastfeeding women and people with chronic disease should not drink. The 2016 edition set limits of 25 g/day for men and 15 g/day for women — [澎湃新闻 2022](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_20291621); [澎湃新闻 2022-12](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_21244425)
- 《中国公民健康素养——基本知识与技能（2024年版）》 (Chinese Citizens' Health Literacy: Basic Knowledge and Skills, 2024 edition) repeats the limit of no more than 15 g of alcohol a day for adults — [同花顺 2026-06](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260609/c677317235.shtml)

**Drink-driving law**
- A blood alcohol concentration of at least 20 mg/100 mL is drink-driving (饮酒驾车), handled administratively. At least 80 mg/100 mL is drunk driving (醉驾). Since the 2011 Criminal Law Amendment (VIII), drunk driving is a criminal offence even if no accident occurs — [Shenzhen Government Online](https://www.sz.gov.cn/en_szgov/news/latest/content/post_11055006.html); [China Daily 2011](https://language.chinadaily.com.cn/cdaudio/2011-05/18/content_12532044.htm)
- The 13 December 2023 opinions issued jointly by the Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, Ministry of Public Security and Ministry of Justice (in force from 28 December 2023) confirm that a BAC of at least 80 mg/100 mL is "dangerous driving" under Criminal Law Article 133-1. Cases with obviously minor circumstances can be exempted from criminal punishment, while aggravating cases such as hit-and-run are punished more severely — [China Law Translate](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/en/spc-spp-opinion-on-drunk-driving/); [China Justice Observer](https://www.chinajusticeobserver.com/a/china-introduces-new-drunk-driving-convictions-standards-effective-2023)
- Enforcement in 2022: 901,000 drink-driving and drunk-driving cases nationwide, of which 177,000 were drunk driving — [gov.cn English, 2023-12-19](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202312/19/content_WS6580f625c6d0868f4e8e2541.html)

### Inferences
- The past-year drinking rate fell from 39.8% (2018) to 27.6% (2024). Together with the 74% fall in baijiu output from 2016 to 2025, this points to a real contraction in Chinese alcohol consumption. Likely drivers include the anti-extravagance rules on official banqueting (八项规定) and their 2025 tightening (the 2025 revision of the rules banning alcohol at official meals), lower business-entertainment demand, population ageing and changing preferences among young people. The policy drivers are inferred, not established by the cited sources.
- The share of current drinkers with HED is still high (over 40%), so the burden of harm will fall more slowly than prevalence. The heavily male drinking pattern (33% of men vs 2% of women in CKB) means harms are concentrated in men.
- Criminalising drunk driving and enforcing it at scale (177,000 drunk-driving cases in 2022) is one of China's most visible alcohol-control measures. Taxation, marketing and availability controls remain relatively weak.

### Gaps
- Prevalence of AUD in China: the China Mental Health Survey (Huang et al., Lancet Psychiatry 2019, n=32,552) did include alcohol use disorders, but the search extracts did not give the alcohol-specific numbers. Commonly cited values are about 4.4% lifetime and about 1.8% over 12 months; these were **not verified** in this session — [Study page](https://colab.ws/articles/10.1016%2FS2215-0366%2818%2930511-X)
- The share of East Asians carrying the ALDH2*2 (rs671) variant (often cited as about 30–40% of Chinese people) and its link to oesophageal cancer were not verified from a source in this session.
- I did not find WHO's 2019 per-capita figure for China (GSR 2024 country profile), GBD estimates of alcohol-attributable deaths in China, or a China-specific economic-cost estimate.

## Q3. National drinking guidelines

### Takeaway
The global trend is to tighten limits. Canada (2023) defines 1–2 standard drinks a week as low risk. Australia sets ≤10 standard drinks a week and ≤4 a day. France sets ≤10 a week and ≤2 a day, not every day. The UK sets ≤14 units a week, spread over at least 3 days. China sets ≤15 g a day. The US 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines go the other way: they **drop the numeric limits** and say only "consume less alcohol". Ireland's cancer warning labels have been postponed from May 2026 to 3 September 2028.

### Cited Findings
- **Canada (CCSA, January 2023):** 0 drinks has no risk, 1–2 drinks a week is low risk, 3–6 is moderate risk (cancer risk rises), and 7 or more is increasingly high risk (heart disease and stroke risk rise). No more than 2 drinks on any one occasion. This replaces the 2011 limits of 10 drinks a week for women and 15 for men — [CCSA poster](https://www.ccsa.ca/sites/default/files/2024-05/Canadas-Guidance-on-Alcohol-and-Health-poster-2023-en.pdf); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6723092)
- **UK (CMO 2016):** no more than 14 units (1 unit = 8 g) a week, spread over at least 3 days, with some alcohol-free days — [Malay Mail factbox](https://malaymail.com/news/life/2025/01/05/from-britain-to-the-us-what-guidelines-do-countries-worldwide-have-on-alcohol-consumption/162144)
- **Australia (NHMRC 2020):** no more than 10 standard drinks (10 g each) a week and no more than 4 on any day. The previous guideline was 2 a day (about 14 a week). People under 18 and pregnant or breastfeeding women should not drink — [MJA 2021](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2021/215/11/revision-australian-guidelines-reduce-health-risks-drinking-alcohol); [Science in Public](https://stories.scienceinpublic.com.au/media-release/alcoholguidelines/)
- **France (Santé publique France / INCa 2017):** at most 10 standard drinks a week and at most 2 a day, not every day — [Santé publique France](https://www.santepubliquefrance.fr/en/alcool/how-can-we-reduce-risks-associated-alcohol-consumption)
- **US (DGA 2025–2030, published 7 January 2026):** removes the long-standing limits of ≤2 drinks a day for men and ≤1 for women, replacing them with the principle "consume less alcohol for better overall health". It keeps the advice that pregnant women and other specific groups should not drink. AASLD criticised the absence of a numeric threshold and of any sex difference. The industry welcomed the change. Reuters reported that the federal Alcohol Intake & Health study, one of the two evidence reviews, was shelved — [AASLD](https://www.aasld.org/aasld-raises-concern-over-removal-evidence-based-alcohol-guidance-2025-2030-dietary-guidelines); [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/us-2025-2030-dietary-guidelines-addressing-alcohol-intake-recommendations); [GV Wire/AP](https://gvwire.com/2026/01/07/trump-administration-ditches-advice-to-limit-alcohol-to-1-2-drinks-per-day/)
- **Ireland labels:** the Public Health (Alcohol) Act 2018 requires cancer and liver-disease warnings on alcohol. The start date was moved from 22 May 2026 to 3 September 2028 (decided July 2025). The government says only the timing has changed. The delay is widely attributed to industry lobbying and to US trade pressure, including the USTR report; this attribution comes from advocacy and trade media — [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/politics/arid-+41673959.html); [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/10/what-we-know-about-irelands-delayed-cancer-warnings-for-alcohol/); [Eurocare](https://www.eurocare.org/eurocare-news/delay-to-irish-alcohol-health-labels-a-setback-but-not-the-end). A study found that voluntary early labelling rose from 6.7% to 8.3% of products — [drugsandalcohol.ie](https://www.drugsandalcohol.ie/46724/)
- **EU:** the European Commission's Knowledge4Policy gateway compiles national low-risk drinking guidelines, and their definitions of a standard drink differ — [EC Knowledge4Policy](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/health-promotion-knowledge-gateway/national-low-risk-drinking-recommendations-drinking-guidelines_en)

### Inferences
- Converted to grams per week: Canada ≤20–27 g (low risk), China ≤105 g (15 g × 7), Australia ≤100 g, France ≤100 g, UK ≤112 g. China's 15 g/day limit is broadly in line with Australia, France and the UK, but far looser than Canada's.
- The US and Ireland cases show that trade, industry and politics are significantly shaping guidelines and labelling in 2025–2026.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full text of the EU's own work on alcohol labelling, the Europe's Beating Cancer Plan or the EU-level outcome of Ireland's labelling notification.
- I did not verify the US Surgeon General's January 2025 advisory on alcohol and cancer, which called for a cancer warning label.

## Q4. WHO policy framework and evidence on effectiveness

### Takeaway
WHO's Global Alcohol Action Plan 2022–2030 sets a **20% relative reduction in per-capita consumption by 2030 (against 2010)**. SAFER is its implementation platform. "Best buys" include raising excise taxes, restricting availability and banning or comprehensively restricting marketing. Natural experiments support this: Scotland's minimum unit pricing (MUP) was followed by a 13.4% fall in wholly alcohol-attributable deaths. After Russia's policies, per-capita consumption fell 43% (2003–2016) and all-cause mortality fell by about 39% in men. After Lithuania's 2017 measures, all-cause mortality fell by about 3.2% beyond trend.

### Cited Findings
- The Global Alcohol Action Plan 2022–2030 was adopted at WHA75 in 2022. Its global target is a 20% relative reduction in per-capita consumption by 2030 compared with 2010. A 30% target proposed by some groups was not adopted — [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/after-a-lost-decade-new-who-alcohol-action-plan-offers-historic-chance-to-address-health-risk-to-billions/); [WMA statement](https://www.wma.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/WHA75-WMA-GAPA-statement-WHO-draft-action-plan-on-alcohol-May2022.pdf)
- SAFER (WHO 2018) covers five areas: Strengthen restrictions on availability; Advance drink-driving countermeasures; Facilitate screening, brief intervention and treatment; Enforce bans or comprehensive restrictions on advertising, sponsorship and promotion; Raise prices through excise taxes and pricing policy. WHO's SAFER progress report says the action plan formally makes SAFER the main implementation platform, and that the SDG alcohol target (3.5.2) is not on track — [WHO SAFER progress report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240120587); [McCabe Centre](https://www.mccabecentre.org/news-and-updates/where-next-for-the-global-governance-of-alcohol-and-public-health.html)
- "Best buys" (WHO NCD Appendix 3): raise excise taxes; enact and enforce bans or comprehensive restrictions on advertising; restrict availability (retail hours and outlets). Brief psychosocial intervention is listed as a cost-effective intervention — [HKMJ](https://www.hkmj.org/system/files/hkmj215109.pdf); [McCabe policy brief](https://www.mccabecentre.org/downloads/Publications/Alcohol-Policy-Brief-FINAL.pdf)
- **Scotland MUP (£0.50/unit from May 2018, raised to £0.65 in 2024):** Wyper et al., Lancet 2023;401:1361–70, controlled interrupted time series against England. In the first 32 months, wholly alcohol-attributable deaths fell 13.4% (95% CI −18.4 to −8.3; p=0.0004) and hospital admissions fell 4.1%. The effect was largest in the four most deprived deciles, an estimated 156 deaths averted a year. Caveats: observational design, and only wholly attributable outcomes were measured — [IAS](https://www.ias.org.uk/news/minimum-unit-pricing-decreases-alcohol-deaths-by-13-in-scotland/); [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/294764); [Science Media Centre](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/?p=50830). (The £0.65 uplift and the 156/year figure are background knowledge and are not in the search extracts.)
- **Russia:** measures from 2003 onwards (minimum prices, night ban on off-premise sales, tighter marketing and availability rules) were followed by a 43% fall in per-capita consumption up to 2016. From 2003 to 2018, all-cause mortality fell 39% in men and 36% in women. Life expectancy reached record highs in 2018: 68 years for men and 78 for women. Consumption is still about 11–12 L. Gains slowed after some rules were relaxed in 2015 — [WHO Europe 2019](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/01-10-2019-alcohol-related-deaths-drop-in-russian-federation-due-to-strict-alcohol-control-measures-new-report-says); [UN News](https://news.un.org/feed/view/en/story/2019/10/1048282)
- **Lithuania (2017 tax increase; 2018 marketing ban, minimum purchase age raised to 20 and shorter sale hours):** Štelemėkas et al., Addiction 2021: in years with effective alcohol policy, all-cause mortality fell about 3.2% more than trend. The estimated 1,452 deaths averted has a 95% CI that crosses zero. Cirrhosis mortality after the 2017 measures fell from 2.85 to 2.01 per 100,000, about 245 deaths averted. Among ages 30–39, mortality fell within 12 months of the policy — [PMC8302690](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8302690); [UKE: cirrhosis study](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/can-alcohol-control-policies-reduce-cirrhosis-mortality-an-interrupted-timeseries-analysis-in-lithuania(f6abb8c6-65cb-4bbe-9224-de85d16a5e9a).html); [Movendi](https://movendi.ngo/science-digest/alcohol-control-policies-reduce-cirrhosis-mortality/)

### Inferences
- Price measures (taxes and MUP) have the strongest causal evidence, and their benefits are concentrated in the most deprived groups. They narrow the health inequalities discussed under the "harm paradox" in Q5.
- The global SDG and GAAP targets are unlikely to be met. The 2010–2019 fall was only about 3.5%, so the remaining 16.5 percentage points would have to happen in a decade, without SAFER coverage in most low- and middle-income countries.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain WHO's latest country-level SAFER implementation data, for example how many countries have an alcohol marketing ban or a minimum price.
- The final Public Health Scotland MUP evaluation report (2023) and the full-period MUP figures were not retrieved.

## Q5. Socioeconomic costs and harm to others

### Takeaway
A global systematic review estimates the economic cost of alcohol at about **2.6% of GDP (95% CI 2.0–3.1%)**, or about Int$1,306 per adult, of which **61% is lost productivity**. Earlier single-country estimates range from 0.45% to 5.44% of GDP. Injury deaths (traffic, violence, self-harm) number about 724,000 a year. The "alcohol harm paradox" means disadvantaged groups suffer more harm at the same or lower consumption, which supports pricing policies that target cheap, high-strength alcohol.

### Cited Findings
- Manthey et al., PharmacoEconomics 2021 (systematic review and modelling, 29 studies): after adjusting for missing cost components, the cost is about Int$1,306 per adult, **2.6% of GDP (95% CI 2.0–3.1%)**. Unadjusted, it is Int$817.6, or 1.5% of GDP. Productivity losses make up 61.2%. The studies are mostly from high-income countries and highly heterogeneous — [UKE portal](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/what-are-the-economic-costs-to-society-attributable-to-alcohol-use-a-systematic-review-and-modelling-study(1cd2dc1b-51fa-44b1-add8-46624ef228c4)/export.html); [Movendi](https://movendi.ngo/science-digest/alcohols-economic-costs-to-society/)
- Rehm et al., Lancet 2009 / systematic review of 12 countries: the economic burden is 0.45%–5.44% of GDP. Baumberg estimates the global burden at about 0.6–2.0% of global GDP — [PMC2791094](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2791094/); [medRxiv 2022](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.04.27.22274363.full.pdf)
- Injuries: of WHO's 2.6 million deaths, about 724,000 are injury deaths from traffic, self-harm and interpersonal violence — [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/urgent-global-action-is-needed-to-address-alcohol-and-drug-consumption/)
- China drink-driving enforcement: 901,000 cases in 2022 — [gov.cn](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202312/19/content_WS6580f625c6d0868f4e8e2541.html)
- Alcohol harm paradox: Boyd et al. (Addiction 2022) systematic review finds that disadvantaged groups have higher alcohol-related harm at similar or lower average consumption. The mechanisms are unclear, and most research has focused on behavioural risk factors — [Stirling hub](https://www.stir.ac.uk/research/hub/publication/2143059). Drinkaware's England data show the same pattern — [Drinkaware](https://www.drinkaware.co.uk/research/research-and-evaluation-reports/drinking-and-deprivation-in-england). The paradox also appears among Danish adolescents (JECH 2019) — [JECH](https://jech.bmj.com/content/73/9/839). Among French 17–18-year-olds, the lowest-SES group is more likely to attend emergency departments because of alcohol — [PubMed 38069928](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38069928/)
- Scotland MUP had its largest effect in the four most deprived deciles (see Q4) — [IAS](https://www.ias.org.uk/news/minimum-unit-pricing-decreases-alcohol-deaths-by-13-in-scotland/)
- Gallup 2026: US drinking rates rise with income (43% below $50k vs 66% at $100k or more), so higher-income groups drink more often while harm is concentrated among the poor — [NACS](https://convenience.org/stay-current/news/2026/august/31/gallup-drinking-remains-at-record-low)

### Inferences
- Applying 2.6% of GDP to China's GDP of about RMB 130–140 trillion would imply annual costs of several trillion yuan. This is an extrapolation only: no direct China estimate was found, and the review's data come mostly from high-income countries.
- Productivity losses (premature death, absenteeism, presenteeism) are the main cost component, rather than healthcare spending. This means fiscal analyses that count only healthcare costs and tax revenue seriously underestimate the net social cost.

### Gaps
- No quantitative global or Chinese estimate of "harm to others" (e.g., Laslett et al.; the WHO/ThaiHealth project) was retrieved.
- I did not obtain the main figures from OECD's *Preventing Harmful Alcohol Use* (2021), such as the reduction in life expectancy and the GDP and fiscal impact.
- No estimate of alcohol-related crime or violence shares (e.g., the alcohol involvement rate in violent crime) or of road-traffic death shares was retrieved; the WHO total injury figure was the only one found.
